Use an Old Mac as a Baby Monitor: Setup Guide and Tips

Published: 14 Nov 2026 · About 5 min read

An old Mac or MacBook in a drawer can be a perfectly good baby monitor with a camera: built-in camera, a decent microphone and permanent power. This guide covers when it makes sense, how to set it up, and where the limits are.

Why a Mac can work as the baby unit

A baby monitor with a camera always has two parts. One device sits in the nursery and captures video and sound (the baby unit). The other stays with you and shows it (the parent unit). The baby unit needs no special hardware: any computer with a camera and a microphone will do, and every Mac has both.

A Mac has a few practical advantages over a phone:

  • Constant power: A MacBook on its charger or an iMac is plugged in anyway, so battery life is not a worry.
  • Stable footing: An open MacBook stands steadily on a shelf or dresser. You do not need a mount.
  • No second phone tied up: The old laptop has nothing else to do, and your phone stays in your hand.
  • External camera possible: On a Mac mini, or a Mac with a poor camera, you can attach a USB webcam.

There are downsides too, covered below. A Mac is bigger, warmer and less flexible to position than a small device.

What you need

  • A Mac running a macOS version the app supports (the requirements are listed on the App Store page).
  • An iPhone or iPad as the parent unit. In LaLeLu the parent unit runs on iPhone and iPad, while the Mac takes the baby unit role.
  • An internet connection on both devices: the same Wi-Fi, or mobile data on the parent side.
  • An Apple iCloud login on both devices. The app has no account of its own but uses iCloud once for pairing.
  • A power adapter and an outlet at a safe distance from the crib.

Step by step: setting up the Mac

  1. Install LaLeLu from the App Store on the Mac and on the iPhone.
  2. Open the app on the Mac and choose Baby unit. On the iPhone choose Parent unit.
  3. Allow camera and microphone access when the Mac asks on first launch. Without these permissions nothing can be transmitted. If you denied them by mistake, you can change them in System Settings under Privacy & Security.
  4. The Mac creates a pairing link or QR code. Open the link on the iPhone or scan the code.
  5. Test the connection in the same room before you set the device up. Speak softly and check that picture and sound arrive.

Important: The pairing link is the key to the connection. Share it only with people you trust, never in public chats.

Sleep and display: keep the Mac awake

The most common stumbling block is sleep. If the Mac goes to sleep, the transmission stops. Check the energy settings before the first night:

  • In System Settings under Energy (on MacBooks: Battery or Power Adapter), set the Mac not to sleep automatically while on the power adapter.
  • The display may go dark. That saves energy and does not disturb the room. What matters is that the Mac itself stays awake.
  • On a MacBook, do not close the lid. A closed laptop without an external display usually sleeps, and the camera is covered anyway.
  • Run a test during a nap before you rely on it overnight.

The exact names of the settings differ between macOS versions.

Placing it safely

A baby monitor does not belong in the crib, and neither do its cables.

  • Distance: Put the Mac out of your baby’s reach, for example on a tall dresser or shelf, tip-proof and away from the edge of the bed.
  • Cables: Keep the adapter and cable out of reach. Long cables near a bed are a hazard, also for older children.
  • Heat: A laptop gives off heat. Do not cover the vents or put it on soft surfaces such as pillows or blankets.
  • Angle: The built-in camera looks straight ahead or slightly up. Check that the bed is in view, and use an external camera if it is not.

Pros and cons, honestly

Pros

No extra hardware cost, stable power, a solid microphone, and an old device gets a useful second life.

Cons

  • A Mac is bulkier than a phone and harder to position.
  • A laptop draws more power than a small device and may make fan noise, depending on the model.
  • The parent unit is an iPhone or iPad. There is no Mac receiver in LaLeLu.
  • Android and Windows devices are not supported.

Not a medical device: A baby monitor, whether an app or a gadget, does not replace supervision or a breathing monitor. It reports sound; it does not track health values. If you have questions about your baby’s safety, talk to your pediatrician.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a MacBook as a baby monitor?

Yes. A MacBook has a camera and microphone and works as the baby unit. Keep it plugged in and open, and prevent sleep. You need an iPhone or iPad as the parent unit.

How do I stop the Mac from going to sleep?

In System Settings under Energy, set the Mac not to sleep automatically while on the power adapter. The display can go dark. Test it before the first night.

Is the built-in camera good enough?

Usually, if the bed is in view. If the angle is poor, many Macs accept a USB webcam. Compatibility and image quality depend on the model.

Does a Mac baby monitor replace a breathing monitor?

No. A baby monitor transmits picture and sound and alerts you to noise. It is not a medical device and does not replace supervision.
